# 📖 Project Story

The global transition from fossil fuels to renewable energy is one of the most important challenges of the 21st century. Governments, industries, and researchers require reliable data to understand how countries are progressing toward cleaner electricity systems and where further action is needed.

While large public datasets contain extensive information, they are often difficult to interpret without advanced analytical tools. Decision-makers need more than raw numbers—they need clear insights, meaningful comparisons, and interactive exploration.

**EnerTrace Atlas** was developed to bridge this gap.

This project transforms the **Our World in Data Energy Dataset** into a comprehensive analytical platform that enables users to explore global renewable energy adoption, electricity carbon intensity, fossil-fuel dependence, long-term transition progress, and future transition strategies through an interactive Streamlit dashboard.

Rather than presenting static charts, EnerTrace Atlas tells the story of how countries are transforming their electricity systems and provides an intuitive environment for exploring that transformation.

# ⚡ Energy Transition Performance Index

One of the major contributions of this project is the development of a custom **Energy Transition Performance Index**.

Instead of ranking countries using only renewable electricity share, the index evaluates multiple dimensions of transition performance.

The index combines:

| Component | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| Renewable Electricity Share | 35% |
| Low Carbon Intensity | 25% |
| Renewable Improvement Since 2000 | 20% |
| Low Fossil Fuel Dependence | 20% |

This balanced scoring framework rewards countries that combine strong renewable adoption with cleaner electricity systems and sustained long-term progress.

> **Note:** This index is an academic analytical framework developed for this project and should not be interpreted as an official international ranking.

# 💡 Key Findings

The analysis reveals several important insights:

- Renewable electricity generation has increased substantially since 2000.
- Countries exhibit very different transition pathways despite similar economic conditions.
- Higher renewable electricity shares are generally associated with lower electricity carbon intensity, although this relationship is not perfectly linear.
- Long-term progress depends not only on current renewable adoption but also on sustained investment and reduced fossil-fuel dependence.
- A multidimensional evaluation provides a more balanced understanding of transition performance than using a single indicator alone.

---

# ⚠ Project Limitations

- The Energy Transition Performance Index is a custom academic metric.
- Rankings depend on the selected indicators and weighting scheme.
- Some countries contain incomplete observations for the most recent years.
- Correlation analysis identifies associations but does not establish causation.
- The project focuses on electricity transition and does not represent the entire energy sector.
